Retreat

Retreats are probably the best way to deepen your practice going forward. Of course not everyone is ready for retreat upon finishing an 8-Week MBSR course, or even ever... but if you've felt the benefits of practice and are curious about going deeper, then we would absolutely recommend thinking about a retreat.

Both of us at Moment to Moment sit retreats at least once a year. Whether 1-day, 3 days, or 10 days, the retreat environment facilitates a space that supports you to bring continuous mindfulness into every moment of the day: sitting, breathing, walking, eating. Each moment is an opportunity to pay careful attention to your experience, and extend your capacity to open to whatever arises.

Self-Understanding

Retreats are usually conducted in silence, creating a stillness and space that supports us in deepening and stabilising our concentration. This allows us to see with greater clarity and steadiness of mind the ways our mind creates unnecessary suffering for us: the storylines we get lost in, the particular feelings and emotions we find it hard to stay present with, the habitual ways we react to experiences. And with the seeing of the particular causes of our suffering, comes the possibility of letting go.

As well as the deepening of our self-understanding, retreat is a perfect opportunity to extend kindness and compassion to ourselves, to understand our own humanity as creatures who suffer, experience inadequacy, feel imperfect, and to learn to care for, appreciate, and cherish ourselves exactly for being this way, i.e. human.

The Nature of Experience

Retreats are a perfect space to observe experience as it is, letting go of all of our ideas and assumptions about experience and discovering the very nature of experience. When our understanding of experience and our responses to it, aren't in alignment with how experience actually is, some friction will be created that ultimately causes us suffering. A retreat offers us the chance to investigate the very nature of experience itself, so that we can reduce the friction between our mind and the world, and live with greater ease. We can inquire into:

What is experience/awareness? What are the general characteristics of experience?

Some examples of the kinds of insights into experience that we can arrive at on retreat are insights into impermanence and selflessness/no-self.

Impermanence

Whatever has the nature to arise, shall also pass away'. Many of us unconsciously assume that a difficult experience may become fixed, permanent and unchanging, e.g. a moment of sadness, which makes us so much more fearful and reactive to it. But experience is constantly changing so we don't need to hold on so tight. We've touched on this a little during the course and maybe you have explored it somewhat during your practice. Retreat is a chance to deepen this awareness and really let this truth of change seep into the very marrow of your bones. Understanding that nothing lasts, pulls the rug from under the reactive, grasping mind and helps us let go. Why are we clinging so tightly to that which is already changing? Why are we so desperately pushing away that which is already passing away?

Selflessness/no-self

What is the nature of this thing we call a 'self'? Is there some fixed, constant and unchanging subject at the centre of our experience, a subject of experience that we can feel somewhere behind our eyes?

This one is admittedly more puzzling. But it is not an intellectual puzzle or some complex idea that we need to get our head around. Just like how we pay attention to our immediate experience to see if experience is fixed or changing, this is just an invitation to pay attention to our immediate experience to see if you can discover a 'subject' of experience over and above the many objects being experienced.

Just asking the question, 'what am I' and inquiring into experience is enough. As well as the thought itself, do we also experience a 'thinker' as part of the experience? Or is there just a bundle of interconnected thoughts, feelings, sensations and perceptions, arising and passing away in the open space of awareness?

Drop out of the level of ideas and assumptions about who we believe to be controlling or orchestrating our thoughts and feelings, and actually take a look at your immediate experience, what do you find? On retreat we are given the opportunity to look for ourselves, literally.

Retreat Centres

Retreats can throw up challenging experiences at times, so finding a supportive retreat environment that feels right for you is really important.

Here are some recommendations from centres we have personally attended: (There are many other retreat centres around the U.K. but we wouldn't want to recommend somewhere that we have no experience of)

Gaia House, Devon. www.gaiahouse.co.uk Verdict: Highly Recommend.

Settle in to a world-renowned buddhist retreat space in an incredible setting, with truly incredible food, and excellent world-renowned teachers and support. Options range from online retreats, silent weekend retreats, to 7 and 10 day silent retreats. Recommend this for anyone from beginner to advanced practitioners. This is just about the deepest and most supportive environment we have found for undertaking a serious meditation retreat. Gaia House does everything it can to not exclude anyone from attending a retreat for financial reasons, and last time we checked that means you can pretty much pay as much as you can afford if you let them know your situation.

Both of us went on retreat together here and it was an incredible experience for us both. And Sam's mum has even attended a retreat here since then!

Sharpham Trust, Devon. www.sharphamtrust.org Verdict: Recommend.

Offer a range of mindfulness retreats, from one day retreats to 5-day retreats. Usually offer a less demanding practice schedule than other retreat centres and it is set in an absolutely breathtaking location so is a great introduction to retreat. On the more expensive end however, oh and they also have incredible food.

Goenka Vipassana Centres in U.K: www.dhamma.org Verdict: Somewhat Recommend.

This is a buddhist meditation centre where you can sit between 3 and 10-day silent retreats here. All the focus is on meditation practice here. It can be pretty spartan, with a bit of a 'bootcamp' vibe about it, very demanding practice schedule and less of a focus on emotional support. Expect little in the way of comforts, but a very serious and deep practice environment to throw yourself into practice at the deep end. The major pull here is financial. They are extremely generous with their retreat offerings running by donations only so you can literally pay whatever you want. (Honestly we would still recommend Gaia House over this as you get an equally deep practice environment but one with greater support and comfort. As we said, retreats can be pretty challenging experiences at times).
