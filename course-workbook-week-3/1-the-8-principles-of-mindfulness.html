The 8 Principles of Mindfulness

There are 8 “grounding principles” of mindfulness which underpin everything we are studying here. We’ve touched on a few of them in our first two weeks together, but it’s worth bringing them all together explicitly in one place. These principles are not abstract ideas but are mental attitudes or qualities that we cultivate through meditation practice; ways of relating to our immediate experience that we can bring to every moment of our lives.

Here they are:

1) Beginner’s mind

This quality of awareness sees everything as new and fresh, as if for the first time, with a sense of curiosity.

2) Non-judgement

This quality of awareness involves cultivating impartial observation in regard to any experience- not labelling thoughts, feelings or sensations as good or bad, right or wrong, fair or unfair, but simply taking note of thoughts, feelings or sensations in each moment.

3) Acknowledgement and Letting be

This quality of awareness validates and acknowledges things as they are without trying to resist, change, or even let go of whatever is present. We allow experience just to be as it is.

4) Non-striving

With this quality of awareness, there is no grasping, aversion to change, or movement away from whatever arises in the moment. When we bring awareness to experience we are not trying to make anything happen, or feel anything in particular. Non-striving means not trying to go anywhere else or be anything other than what you are in each moment.

5) Equanimity

This quality of awareness is an inner balance or steadiness of mind that arises from an understanding of change. When we see into the changing, impermanent nature of things, we are no longer tossed around so much by each passing experience, instead finding balance and even-mindedness in the midst of life.

6) Patience

Patience is a form of wisdom. It demonstrates that we understand and accept the fact that sometimes things must unfold in their own time. To be patient is to open yourself to each moment, not expecting anything in particular, but accepting each moment in its fullness.

7) Trust

Developing a basic trust in yourself and your feelings is an integral part of meditation training. It is far better to trust in your own intuition and authority than looking outside to others. We also develop trust in the process of meditation, relaxing the need to validate our growth by seeking reward and improvement from every sit.

8) Self-compassion

This quality of awareness cultivates love for yourself as you are, without self-blame or criticism. Being able to hold your experience with kindness and compassion may be just about the most supportive thing you can ever really do for yourself.

Holding these qualities in mind- reflecting upon them and cultivating them according to your best understanding will nourish, support and strengthen your practice. Developing these qualities is a way of channelling your energies into the process of healing and growth. These attitudes are all interdependent; mutually influencing one another, so by cultivating one you strengthen them all. Since practice cultivates these attitudes in us, as well as being the principles of mindfulness you could also describe them as the benefits of mindfulness practice.
