The half day retreat

The retreat sits between weeks six and seven, and it is part of the course rather than an extra. Its purpose is simple: to give you an extended stretch of practice, mostly in silence, using everything you have learned so far. Many people describe it as the point where the practices stop being things they have been taught and start being something they know their way around. (See also: 2. Retreat, which is about going on retreat after the course.)

Practicalities

  • Set aside roughly half a day, ideally a morning, around three hours.
  • Choose a time when you will not be disturbed. Tell the people you live with, or put a note on the door.
  • Phone off, or in another room. This is the hardest instruction and the most important one.
  • Wear comfortable clothes. Have water within reach.
  • You will be moving between practices, so set up a space with enough room to stand and stretch, and a place to sit.
  • If you are doing the retreat as part of a live course, it may be guided. If you are doing it alone, the schedule below works unguided: just move through it, keeping an eye on a clock or setting gentle alarms between practices.

Schedule

Time Practice
15 mins Arriving: settle in, sit, and let the mind arrive in the room. Mindful breathing.
30 mins Body scan.
20 mins Mindful movement.
15 mins Walking practice, or a slow mindful walk around your home or garden, half of it at ordinary speed, half at half that speed.
25 mins Sitting: awareness of thoughts and emotions.
10 mins Break. Walk, stretch, drink water. Do not read, check the phone, or start a task. The break is also the practice.
20 mins Sitting: loving kindness.
20 mins The mountain practice.
15 mins Sitting: choiceless awareness.
20 mins Eating practice: eat one simple meal or snack in complete silence and full attention. Notice everything: hunger, the first taste, the fading of interest, fullness.
15 mins Closing: sit, and let the retreat end deliberately. Take a few minutes to notice how you feel, and to set an intention for the rest of the day.

A few things to expect

The retreat is usually where the mind puts up the biggest fight. Expect restlessness around the halfway point, an urgent conviction that you are wasting your time, and a parade of very convincing reasons to stop. You have met all of these before, at smaller scale, during practice at home. The retreat simply makes them louder. Each time you notice and return, that is the practice, and there is nowhere else you are meant to get to.

Emotionally, the retreat can also be where things surface: sadness, unexpected memories, an upwelling of something unnamed. If that happens, treat it exactly as you would in sitting practice: with awareness, gentleness, and the breath. If something feels overwhelming, stop, walk, get some air. The retreat is a practice, not an ordeal.

Silence is a core part of the retreat. Not because silence is holy, but because it removes the usual escapes. For half a day, awareness has fewer places to hide. Most people find, by the end, that the silence was the best part.

You have practised in stolen minutes all these weeks. The retreat is the first time practice gets room to breathe. Let it.
