Walking Meditation

Welcome to the walking meditation

The more we can find ways to extend our practice into different activities like walking, the more we build momentum and continuity in our mindfulness, making a habit of awareness. Practices like walking meditation act as this important bridge between our formal sitting practice and our time spent out and about in the world, supporting us to be more awake, and better able to respond skilfully to the ups and downs of our daily lives.

As with mindful movement and the body scan practices, the aim of this practice is to deepen our awareness of the body. As we walk, we will simply pay attention to the changing flow of physical sensations in our bodies, grounding ourselves in these physical sensations. And each time the mind wanders, we simply return to these sensations.

The beauty of this practice is you can do it almost anywhere. Of course if you want to use it as a way of getting outdoors then great. You might take this practice outside on a walk in the park or in nature, or on a walk you do each day, maybe to work, or to the shops. But even if you are indoors, just find an unobstructed space of about 5 strides and do the practice there.

This recording will last around 10 minutes but feel free to extend or shorten this practice as you feel.

Before we begin walking, just taking a few moments to come into your experience of the body in this moment. Feeling the weight of the body, feeling the support of the ground beneath you, pushing you up by the soles of the feet. Feeling into this connection with the earth beneath you at the feet. Feeling any subtle movements in the feet as the body naturally orients itself towards balance.

Now start walking, it helps to walk a little slower than usual, taking clear slow steps, and if you are indoors you might walk a touch slower than that even, but not so much that it feels difficult to balance.

As you begin walking, bring your awareness to the soles of the feet, feeling into the physical sensations as each foot makes contact with the ground, sensations of pressure, hardness, heat, movement. Noticing what is here.

Really being curious. Can you bring a beginners mind to walking, as if noticing what it is like for the first time.

If the mind wanders at any time into thought and emotion as it will, noticing this and returning once again to the immediate sensations at the feet as you walk.

If you are indoors and need to turn around, not rushing this movement, but again staying connected to the body at the soles of the feet as you feel into the physical sensations as you turn around and begin walking in the other direction.

We aren't going anywhere here, there's no destination we're trying to get to, simply walking, one step at a time. Receiving the sensations of walking as you place one foot after the other.

Now noticing what it feels like as the foot lifts, moves and then lowers into the next step. Feeling into the sensations as you lift, move and place each foot. lift, move, step, lift, move step.

Sensing into this flow of changing sensations in the body. Allowing the mind to settle into the sensations of walking as they are right now.

Sensing now also into the legs as you are walking, feeling any sensations in the muscles of the lower legs, tightening and softening as the foot is lifted and lowered.

Being playful with the practice, if you feel like it, you might explore walking at different speeds, perhaps slowing down and magnifying your awareness on the intricacies of each step, or speeding up a bit and noticing more of the flow of experience, aware of any changes in both the body and the mind as you do this.

Noticing also any reactions in the mind as you notice the sensations of walking. Do you have subtle any preferences here? Perhaps you are more drawn to the sensations in a particular leg, or in a particular part of the movement? Or maybe some sensations are more pleasant than others?

Can we let go of any preferences here, staying completely open to each sensation in the body? Now it's like this, now it's like this.

If the mind gets lost in thought or emotion, bringing it back to the immediate sensations of the body moving over the earth. Noticing what it is like once again to be here, to be alive, to be grounded in our awareness of the body walking, as if noticing it for the first time.

Now you can get as curious and inquisitive as you like here, exploring as much of the body as you are drawn to. Feeling into any parts of the body that are involved in walking, feeling the arms and hands swinging naturally by your sides, the hips, the abdomen, even noticing the head bobbing through space.

Just how remarkably complicated is this apparently ordinary and mostly unconscious act of walking.

Offering a sense of gratitude perhaps to the body, for the effortlessness in which the it executes this complex pattern of movements, keeping you steady and balanced as you move through space.

Perhaps if it feels right for you, extending this gratitude to all that the body does and all that it gives you.

For the last few minutes of the guided part of this practice, you might open your awareness out to the sights and sounds of your surroundings. Keeping awareness anchored in the body whilst also allowing sounds, sights, and whatever else to come to you, as you walk over this earth. Allowing the mind to rest in the awareness that's already here, of the body, of sounds, sights, thoughts, emotions, letting things be as they are.

This ends the walking meditation practice, feel free to continue walking or whatever it is you choose to do next, can you take with you this renewed awareness of your experience moment after moment.

Thank you for your practice.
