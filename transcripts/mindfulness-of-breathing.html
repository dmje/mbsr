Mindfulness of the breath

Welcome to the mindfulness of breath practice.

In this mindfulness of the breath practice we will be exploring the physical sensations of the breath as it moves through the body. The breath is always with us, so it can help to anchor our awareness to the present moment as we return to it over and over again.

This practice is also an opportunity to notice any mental habits, of reactivity, or patterns of thought that can hinder us or cause us suffering. There will be times when the mind gets lost, in thoughts, emotions or discomfort in the body, and so this practice is also an invitation to bring qualities of kindness and compassion towards ourselves.

The practice will last around 30 minutes - so try to find a time and place where you can be still and uninterrupted for its duration.

We’ll be doing the practice in relative stillness so make sure you are warm enough, perhaps putting a blanket or loose covers over you if that feels necessary.

This is a longer seated practice, so let’s take a few moments to intentionally settle into our sitting posture. Our sitting posture provides a crucial balance between wakefulness in the mind and stillness in the body. If we are too relaxed then we can become sleepy, but if we are too tense then we cannot settle the mind.

If you are sitting on a chair bring your back away from the chair to support yourself in an upright position. Try to find a sense of uprightness in the spine, without being stiff or rigid. To find the sweet spot here, imagine yourself being gently pulled up from the crown of your head by a piece of string attached to the ceiling, bringing into alignment your head, spine and tailbone.

Of course, listen to your body here, do adapt things to suit your body’s specific needs. If at any time you experience intense discomfort, feel free to adjust your position, but try to do so mindfully and as a continuation of the practice.

Let's begin by allowing the the eyes to close, or if it feels more comfortable you can keep them open with a soft unfocused gaze. Taking a few deep breaths to settle the mind. and once the breath has returned to its natural rhythm bring your attention to the body sitting, feeling the weight of the body in the sitting posture. Feel any points of contact with the floor or chair, perhaps at the sit bones, or the soles of the feet. Grounding yourself in this connection with the earth beneath you.

Now shifting our awareness to the rest of the body, noticing any areas of tension or tightness in the body, softening around them. Perhaps at the shoulders, the jaw or around the eyes, noticing your experience as it is.

Noticing also any emotions that you might be holding onto in the body. Maybe around the heart area, or at the belly. Can we soften around this too?

Checking in with the attitude in the mind. Is there striving in the mind? Or is there struggle and resistance? Taking a moment just to put down any plans, goals or expectations about the practice. Settling into this moment right here, right now, with the intention just to practice. (cut this at least the first bit)

Now noticing the body breathing. Allowing the breath to be natural. Feeling any larger movements as the body moves with the breath. Maybe you can feel a sense of the whole body lifting and lowering as the breath comes in and out. Perhaps you can feel the breath in the shoulders or the back of the body, expanding as it fills the lungs before returning to softness. Feeling into these larger movements for a few moments.

And if the mind wanders at any time, noticing without judgement where the mind has gone, any thoughts and feelings that have arisen before bringing our awareness back to the sensations of the body breathing with kindness.

Let the breath come to you. Receiving the breath, you don’t need to do anything at all here, the body breathes itself, can you simply notice the breath with awareness, moment after moment.

Now settling your attention on an area of the body where you can feel the breath most obviously. Perhaps at the nostrils, or at the belly, or chest. It doesn't matter where you choose, just settle on a place that the felt sensations of breathing are most clear to you.

lightly resting your attention at this place, steadying the mind on the sensations at this area of the body.

Becoming sensitive to the breath, taking an interest in the breath. Does it appear to flow smoothly, or is it more uneven. Is the breath short or long? Perhaps there are sensations of coolness and warmth at the nostrils, or tingling, pressure or movement, in the belly or chest.

Staying open to each breath, becoming curious. As the breath comes in, how do you know the breath is coming in? And as the breath goes out, how do you know the breath is going out? 

We're not thinking about the breath here, or knowing it in a conceptual way, just feeling the raw physical sensations of the breath moving through the body.

If the mind is caught in thinking, letting go of any judgement as you become aware of any thoughts in the mind. The mind thinks, that’s what minds do, accept this too as part of the landscape of your experience, and part of the practice. Each time the mind wanders is an equally important part of the practice, it is a chance to renew our awareness of the present moment, to strengthen our mindfulness muscle, and a chance to practice bringing kindness to ourselves. So once again, returning to the breath.

Can we reconnect with that childlike curiosity in things. Exploring the mystery of the breath, no two breaths are ever the same. Can we receive each breath in its fullness with awareness, settling back and staying open.

The breath is always with us, as an anchor guiding us back to the moment. Over and over again we take refuge in the breath, each time the mind becomes distracted, return to the breath.

Not leaning into the next breath, can we let this breath be enough, just this breath.

And If the mind is turbulent, with a tangle of thoughts and mixed emotions keeping us from remaining with the breath for any length of time, that’s ok. Sometimes the mind is still, sometimes the mind is not. Can we simply let thoughts come and go, like trains passing through a station, not needing to get onboard the story of each one. Allowing the mind to be as it is, watching thoughts pass through the mind, unobstructed, without getting in the way.

Watching the breath, noticing thinking, letting go with kindness and beginning again.

There will be times when our experience becomes more difficult. Can we ask ourselves, how am I in relation to my experience? Is there grasping in the mind, or am I pushing anything away? Perhaps tyring to change my expeience? Can we simply notice this tendency to want things to be other than they already are, with compassion. However your experience in this moment, can you simply be with it, observing it without judgement, holding it with kindness. Can you let this moment be enough, just as it is.

And when you feel ready, reconnecting once again with the breath. Settling back into the experience of breathing, letting the rhythm of the breath soften the mind.

Each experience is just a visitor, like all things that arise, they also pass away in time, change is the very nature of things. If difficult thoughts are there, strong emotions or unpleasant sensations in the body, can we welcome each experience as you would a visitor, shaking its hand with friendliness, knowing that soon it too will be gone.

Now for the last minute or so of the practice, let go of any effort, of any deliberate focus of attention, and allow the mind just to rest in the way things are moment after moment. Settle back into the fullness of your experience, just as it is, leaving everything in its own place, resting the mind in the open and spacious nature of awareness.

Now bringing the awareness to the body sitting, feeling into any points of contact with the earth. Noticing also how we are feeling at the close of the practice. Taking a few moments to offer yourself kindness for the care you are giving to yourself and letting any benefits of the practice sink deep into the marrow of your bones. And gently opening the eyes, Perhaps wiggling the fingers or toes. Taking as much time as you need, before continuing with your day.

This ends the mindfulness of breathing practice. Thank you for your practice.
