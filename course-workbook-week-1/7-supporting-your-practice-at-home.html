Supporting your 'Home Practice'

Building a Habit

Consistency of practice is the key to experiencing its benefits. In order to undergo the brain changes that occur from a mindfulness meditation practice, the evidence suggests a practice of over 20 minutes each day is required. We will help you as much as we can to build your practice into a habit over the course of these 8 weeks. It's reckoned that habit-forming takes around about 65 days, so after 8 weeks of daily practice, you'll be pretty much there!

It's worth thinking hard about how best to fit your practice into your day. How you fit it in will depend mostly on the particular shape of your life, however, exploring or at least thinking about some of the different options may help you find a way that is most sustainable.

Here are some suggestions:

Mornings

Do you already have a morning routine that you could tack your practice onto? Are you an early riser? Are your evenings simply too busy, activity-filled or chaotic? Is 'starting the day off on the right foot' something that you resonate with?

Challenges:

Regular sleep difficulties. After a poor night's sleep, getting up early for your meditation practice might be the first thing your overtired mind scraps.

Evenings

Do you struggle to get up early? Are your mornings simply too busy, chaotic or rushed? Do you already have an evening routine you could tack your practice onto? Do you find it helpful to 'decompress' in the evenings?

Challenges:

Meditating too late at night. It can be tempting to put it off until you are right about to go to bed, however, this may cause you to fall asleep during practice. Firstly, this can affect the quality of your practice, and secondly it can develop into a habit causing you to fall asleep during meditation no matter what time of day. Meditation is a great way to help with sleep, however, it is important to keep distinct our formal meditation practice from a sleep meditation.

Whenever?

There is a strange gravitational power to 'routine' or 'ritual' that removes some of the effort involved in consciously having to make a decision to do something. When we are already stressed out, with busy, overactive minds, this effortful decision making requires energy that we just sometimes don't have. With a routine, you have already done the hard work and carved out the time in the day, so it doesn't feel like such a 'decision', you just need to follow the routine and practice.

Ultimately, though, we really encourage you just to practice, so if you aren't a 'routine' kind of person and it means fitting it in wherever you can, then that's absolutely fine. Using 'reminders' will be a huge help to you (see below). Just be honest with yourself about your willingness and commitment to practice each day.

Is it Sustainable?

We want to find the path of least resistance here. So it's no use just considering when you would ideally like to fit your practice into your day. We need to consider what is most realistic and sustainable for you.

For example, if you want to get up earlier, and you think starting a morning meditation practice will help you in this, great! But also think about if you can realistically sustain this? If you don't get up early one morning, will that also mean you won't meditate that day?

You might now be giving yourself two challenges instead of just one.

This requires some self-knowledge, so if you don't already have a good idea of what would work for you, then experiment! Try out different times and see how it feels.

As always try to be honest with yourself if something isn't working or you are really having to force it, even if you might like things to be that way.

Reminders

It's easy to get so tied up in your day that you just forget anything at all about mindfulness or meditation. You get too wrapped up in the stressful momentum and autopilot of your usual daily grind - and then you realise at the end of the day how mindless you've been... and now its late and you are just too tired to sit and practice.

We get it, this is life, and this is why you are here! But this is also how you can lose touch with your intention and commitment to practice.

This is where reminders come in handy. Try some of these to nudge you back on track:

  1. Set reminders on your phone, or download specific apps like OMM (One moment meditation) that ping you a little reminder to practice. You can set the reminder to alert you around the time of your formal practice, or ping you throughout the day to help support you to be more mindful.
  2. Sticker it up! Be creative about how to hack your mind into practicing. Put PostIt's around your house reminding you to meditate. As the course progresses you'll see just how helpful these can be in supporting you to be more mindful. Whether on your bedside table, in the bathroom, or any other place that you know will trigger you to practice.

Boundaries

The biggest challenge you'll face in keeping up a meditation practice is your own mind. We know from experience how much of a mental battle it can be to keep at your practice. Meditation can be uncomfortable and challenging at times and it can take some time before you feel the effect it is having on your life. This can create inner conflict and struggle - thoughts and feelings of self-judgement and insecurity that push you away from practice - and before you know it you've persuaded yourself 'it's not working' or 'this just isn't for me' and meditation becomes 'that thing I tried once'.

This is something we will try to help you with as much as we can as we move through the 8 weeks. We want everyone to feel like they can speak to us and each other. Be open if you are struggling, because probably it's not just you. There is no judgement here.

When we are experiencing struggle in our practice we can look for any excuse not to practice. For example, how might your partner asking you to watch a movie right before your usual time of practice affect your ability to make the decision to practice?

We want to encourage you to set boundaries with those closest to you, to help reduce the risk of external interferences that can affect your ability to practice. Take a moment to think about who and what in your life place the greatest demands on you and your time - a partner, children, family, friends, work, housemates. What would it be like to set a boundary with them for these 8 weeks, that your time for meditation practice is something non-negotiable that, barring an emergency, they have to respect and help support you in?
