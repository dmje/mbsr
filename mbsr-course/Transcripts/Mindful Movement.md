# Mindful Movement

What follows is a guided movement meditation. For this practice we will move through some seated and standing postures.

You'll want to have a chair nearby and enough space to the front back and sides to move your arms up alongside the body.

If standing is not workable for your body you can also practise the standing postures seated in a chair.

Practising mindful movement helps us to cultivate awareness of the body. The body can be a refuge; a place for the attention to rest. When we practise moving mindfully we can also learn to become more aware of the limits of our bodies, get to know them, and practise meeting them with kindness. In the process we might actually expand our limits, strengthen the body and become more physically and mentally flexible.

Follow along with this recording as best you can. If there are any movements that aren't right for you at this time, simply refrain from them, modify them, or take a resting pose, coming back to the breath.

There is no right way to do this practice. The way to do the movements is the way that is right for you. The practice of mindful movement is to be with the body moving, awake to any sensations or thoughts that arise from moment to moment.

If as we practice you notice things that are painful or uncomfortable, you can work with them in the way that is right for you. It might be best to ease off, to find a resting pose – or if it isn’t a harmful pain, you might choose to explore it with your attention, noticing it’s qualities, its shape, its boundaries – or if it changes as you focus on it.

So – come into a standing posture if this is ok for you, placing your feet about a hip-width apart, your arms hanging at your sides.

If you would rather do this exercise sitting down, that is ok too.

If you like you can close your eyes, allowing any unnecessary tension in the body to be let go of – softening the belly, the shoulders, the belly, the face. Be aware of your feet making contact with the floor.

Be aware of the weight of your arms hanging at the sides, aware of breath moving into and out of the torso.

As we continue, you can choose to keep your eyes closed, or open them.

On an inhale, slowly bring the arms up in front of the body, to horizontal and then all the way up so that they are pointing at the ceiling. Feel the stretch in the arms, and in the shoulders. Feel the body lengthening as you reach for the sky. Feel the grounding in your feet even as you lengthen.

With the next in-breath, reach slightly higher with your left hand, feeling the stretch along that side of your body as you do so. With an exhale, allow this side to relax but keep your arm above your head. Now with the next in-breath, reach slightly higher with your right hand, feeling the stretch on that side.

When you exhale, allow this hand to relax again so both are still above your head but equal.

For the next few breaths, reach with alternate sides, reaching higher with one side on the in-breath, then allowing that side to relax, and reaching higher with the other on the next in-breath and allowing that too to relax.

Be aware of what reaching feels like in the arms, hands, sides or maybe in the rest of your body. Notice whether one side is different in any way to the other. Is it easier, or harder, more challenging for one side or the other, or are they both the same?

On an exhale, slowly float your arms back down alongside your body, being with this movement all the way down from stretched to relaxed, noticing the entire movement as you do so, feeling how it is in the arms, hands or elsewhere in the body as you do so.

When the arms reach the sides, close your eyes if they were open and use this focus to be even more aware of the feelings in your body – what is like to have just moved? What is your breath doing – allow it to flow freely, noticing if you have been holding it at all during this first simple exercise.

After a moment, and again on an inhale, move your arms in the same way, floating them slowly and gently upwards with the in-breath. Notice how this feels this time – is it the same, or different? Does it make a difference having done this exercise already – are you expecting the movement now, or is it the same? There is no right or wrong, but just notice how this experience is the same or different.

Now we’ll move gently into a side-bend. Bring your hands together over your head. Place your palms against each other in a prayer-like shape if this is comfortable, or clasp them if you prefer.

With an inhale, gently and slowly bend to the left. Only go as far as you feel comfortable with – remember that there is no right or wrong, no aim or goal apart from noticing the sensations. Focus on what it feels like to be in a gentle crescent moon shape. Breath normally and, if you can, maintain the shape, noticing how the inbreath and outbreath feels. Does your breath stretch a particular side? Can you feel it in a particular place – in your hands or arms or elsewhere in your body?

Slowly with an inhale return to vertical. See if you can feel the afterglow of the stretch.

Now with the next inhale, gently bend to the right. Again, only go as far as you are comfortable with. Breath normally here, again noticing what the breath does to your sensation – how does it change your feeling in your arms, hands and elsewhere – or maybe it makes no difference at all. Just notice, with equanimity and without judgement.

Again, come back up to centre on an inhale.

On an exhale, allow the arms to float back down to your sides. Rest here for a few breaths. Notice what it feels like to have done this exercise. Notice any sensations in the body, any effects of the movements we’ve practiced so far.

Now we’ll do some shoulder rolls. Gently bring both shoulders up towards the ears. Squeeze the shoulder down, forwards, up, down, back – roll them gently around at a pace that feels comfortable for you. Make the movement big if you like, or keep it small – or if you feel uncomfortable, just be still – allow your own body to be the judge of what feels right for you.

Rest your attention in your shoulders, noticing any sensations here.

Notice also any mental reflections as you do these movements. Do you feel mental resistance or a sense of satisfaction as you manage to complete a movement? Are you casting your mind forwards and thinking that this coming movement may be uncomfortable, and resisting in some way? Notice when you are judgemental in any way, when you feel that you should be able to do something and can’t, for example. Think around this judgement, being gentle and compassionate with yourself at all times.

If your attention wanders, gently bring it back. Be with your body, right here and right now.

Now begin to roll your shoulders in the opposite direction, doing it at your own pace and as always in a way that feels comfortable for you.

Do this for a few more breaths, and then on an exhale, allow your shoulders to come to rest. Notice what it feels like to stop. See if you can feel into any remnants from the practice. Place your attention into the arms, shoulders and back, noticing any sensations here or elsewhere in the body.

We’re now going to gently focus on changing our balance.

If you are standing, very slowly shift your weight into your left hand side. Do this with attention. Keep both feet on the floor, just very gently bring perhaps 10% more weight into the left hand side.

If you are seated, just press a little more with your left foot.

Feel what this is like in your feet, in your legs, or in your upper body. Notice any tension, any ways in which you are over-focused on a particular sensation. Allow any tension to drain away, and make your attention wide – notice sensations in your whole body as much as you can.

Now shift a little further. If it is comfortable for you, gently and slowly shift all your weight onto your left hand side. Do this very slowly, imagining a dial showing the weight on your left side increasing very gradually from 10% ….to 50%.... to 100%...

If you can, lift the right foot from the floor a tiny amount. Don’t do anything that makes you uncomfortable – be aware of your limits, and notice any frustrations – remember, there is nothing right or wrong here; if you do experience any frustration or sense of struggle, judgement or competitiveness here just notice that and try to hold it with kindness and perhaps a sense of humour if you can.

If you want to, you can move your right foot a little in a way that feels comfortable – wriggle it, or gently rotate it around the ankle. Notice the sensations in your ankle, your legs, your lower back or elsewhere.

Now gently lower your foot if you had it raised, and very slowly shift your weight back to the centre so that you are equally balanced on both feet. Notice what this feels like. Are there residual feelings in the muscles in your legs or elsewhere now you have come back to the centre? What does this feel like? Is there a feeling of relief at being back in equilibrium or does it feel no different to before?

Let’s repeat this on the other side. As before, do this extremely slowly, bringing your attention as you shift your weight onto your right hand side. Again, just do this a small amount to start off with, maybe 10%, just resting here and noticing what it feels like in the feet, legs or elsewhere. Now shift a little further, listening to your body all the time and making sure you don’t do anything that makes you uncomfortable. Imagine the dial again, moving the weight onto your right hand side in an incremental way from 10% to 50% and then to 100%, finally putting all your weight into your right side.

Gently lift your left foot from the floor if you can, just a tiny amount, and notice the sensations of doing so. Can you feel the muscles working in your foot, or your legs or back? What does it feel like to be balanced on one side rather than the other? Is this the same or different to the opposite side?

Again, try wriggling or otherwise moving your left foot or toes. What is this like?

Now gently lower your foot again, and slowly transfer your weight back to centre – again doing this slowly and with mindfulness, until you are balanced equally on both feet. As before, notice what it feels like to be without movement, back in balance, an equal weight on both sides. Notice if there is any echo of the movement you have just done – can you feel your muscles relaxing or changing as they come to stillness?

Be still for a few breaths.
