# STOP

Mindfulness practice engages the PNS to soothe and calm our nervous system, and allows us to practice a new way of relating to our experience, one of less judgement, and reactivity and of greater creativity and connectivity. Whilst our 30 minutes of formal practice each day works wonders in this regard, we can benefit tremendously from integrating shorter, more on-the-go practices into our daily life. These practices are so important for helping us integrate our work on the cushion into our day to day life, particularly when faced with difficult experiences.

STOP is a quick and easy to remember technique to cut through autopilot and engage our PNS when facing stress. STOP is a really simple technique to put some distance between something in your daily life which normally triggers a “fight or flight” (SNS) type reaction – a stressful phone call, an annoying email from a client, etc – and your response to that reaction.

STOP is powerful because it’s simple to remember and can be used anywhere – no-one needs to know that you’re in the middle of a STOP process: it’s quick, doesn’t require you do do anything “meditative” like closing your eyes, and can be brought to bear on many stressful situations.

The acronym stands for:

- **S** top and stand still
- **T** ake a breath or two
- **O** bserve your thoughts, feelings, experiences and emotions
- **P** roceed mindfully

You may have noticed that although this is nice and quaint and easy to remember, we’re also sneakily activating a physiological shortcut. By using STOP – by pausing, by taking a mindful breath, by observing our experience – we are kicking online our parasympathetic nervous system, the PNS.

We’re inviting a more rested state to come in to our bodies, giving ourselves time to pause, gather ourselves and respond differently or more creatively.

Our suggestion is that you practice STOP as much as you can during this week – and something you try to build into your daily routine as much as possible moving forward in your mindful journey!
