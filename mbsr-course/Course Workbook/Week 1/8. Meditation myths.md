# Ten myths about meditation

Before your first week of practice, it helps to clear away some of the things people wrongly believe about meditation. Most people arrive carrying at least a few of these, and each of them can quietly sabotage a perfectly good practice.

1. **"Meditation means emptying your mind."** It does not. It means noticing what your mind is doing. Thoughts will come, constantly, and that is fine. The practice is in noticing them and beginning again.

2. **"I can't meditate because I can't stop thinking."** This is the same myth in different clothes. If you noticed you were thinking, that noticing was mindfulness. You are not failing; you are doing the practice.

3. **"Meditation is relaxing, so if I don't feel relaxed it isn't working."** Sometimes meditation is relaxing. Sometimes it is restless, boring, frustrating or moving. All of it is practice. Relaxation is a frequent side effect, not the goal.

4. **"There is one correct posture, and I can't do it."** There is no correct posture. There are useful ones: a reasonably upright seat, dignity, staying awake. A chair is as legitimate as a cushion.

5. **"Meditation is religious."** This course is entirely secular. The practices here are drawn from a long tradition, but they are taught as a skill for paying attention, and nothing in this course requires you to believe anything at all.

6. **"If it isn't blissful, I must be doing it wrong."** Meditation experiences range widely. Expecting bliss is a reliable way to feel like a failure. Aim to be present, not transported.

7. **"It takes years to get anywhere."** Research shows measurable changes in weeks with regular practice. But "getting anywhere" is the wrong frame anyway: the practice is the being here, not the arriving.

8. **"I need a quiet place, half an hour, and the right conditions."** Formal practice benefits from a regular time and place, but a meditation that includes the neighbour's dog and the boiler is not an inferior meditation. Awareness of noise is awareness.

9. **"Mindfulness will fix my problems."** Mindfulness changes your relationship to your problems, which often changes the problems themselves. It is not a cure, and promising yourself one is a setup for disappointment.

10. **"I have to do it perfectly or not at all."** A missed day is a missed day, not a broken practice. The whole skill is returning without drama. You will get endless opportunities to practise exactly that.

> One of these myths is probably yours. Keep an eye out for it during the body scan this week; it will show up.
