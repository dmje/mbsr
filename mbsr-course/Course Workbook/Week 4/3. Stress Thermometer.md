# Stress Thermometer

Thermometer Exercise: The thermometer represents different levels of stress, from ‘no stress’ to ‘extreme’. 

Reflect on the experiences associated with each level of stress, as well as their causes.

1. Note down the kinds of events that trigger each level of stress, e.g. running late to work, a bad night’s sleep, physical injury, difficult communications, etc.
2. Note down the body parts and felt sensations in the body that you experience at each level of stress.
3. Note down the emotional and psychological responses associated with each level of stress, e.g. catastrophising, emotional volatility, excessive rumination, anxiety, self-judgement, self-preoccupation etc.

Now consider any practices or actions that could help you mitigate the stress response for each level of stress. 

Side note – notice if there are any feedback effects, for example between the bodily and the psychological or emotional responses. Can these feedback effects cause your stress level to rise too?
