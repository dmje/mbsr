# Neurological Benefits of Mindfulness Practice

There are a wealth of benefits to mindfulness practice, however, with cutting edge scientific research we are now able to see the effects that mindfulness practice has on the brain. Goleman and Davidson in their book “The Science of Meditation” (2017) give us a nice summary of the scientifically grounded benefits we can expect from just the beginnings of a mindfulness meditation practice:

> “From the beginning hours, days and weeks of meditation, several benefits emerge. For one, beginners’ brains show **less amygdala reactivity to stress**. Improvements in attention after just two weeks of practice include **better focus**, **less mind-wandering**, and **improved working memory**… Some of the earliest benefits are with compassion meditation, including **increased connectivity in the circuitry responsible for empathy**. And markers for **inflammation lessen** with just thirty hours of practice.” LINK AT BOTTOM OF PAGE. 

Let's have a closer look on stress.

As we have seen, overly stressed out brains are the product of an overactive amygdala that keeps triggering our sympathetic nervous system. The reason why mindfulness meditation and the MBSR course specifically is so successful in reducing stress is because it has been shown to **reduce the activity of the amygdala**. 

Mindfulness practice creates neural circuitry in the brain that bypasses the amygdala’s stress-response altogether. It teaches us new ways of functioning and interacting with the world that won't engage the circuitry of our amygdala. For example:

1. We learn how to engage the PNS to quieten amygdala activity and return us to a rested, relaxed state, e.g. through abdominal breathing, slowing down the breath, or just connecting to the body. Continuous stimulation of our PNS dials down our stress response over time, bringing our nervous system back into balance. 
2. We also learn how to stay present and connected to our body in the moment, so that we don't automatically reach for a storyline that can trigger strong emotions and engage the amygdala. 
3. Being mindful increases our awareness of the causes or triggers of stress in our lives. Awareness of what causes us stress allows us to intentionally plan our lives more carefully to reduce our exposure to these kinds of situations and better care for our well-being.
