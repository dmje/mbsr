# Loving kindness

So far, the course has trained attention: the breath, the body, thoughts, emotions, other people. Loving kindness practice, sometimes called metta, trains something just as important: the heart's response to all of it, starting with your own.

## The idea in a sentence

Loving kindness practice is the deliberate repeating of simple wishes, first for yourself and then widening outwards, and the noticing of whatever those wishes meet in you.

## The phrases

Use four phrases, simple enough to say in one breath. You can adjust the words so they sound like something you would actually say:

- May I be safe.
- May I be well.
- May I be at ease.
- May I be at peace.

Saying them is not the whole practice, but a beginning. The practice is noticing what happens when you say them.

## The sequence

**1. Yourself.** Begin by bringing yourself to mind, just as you are today. Repeat the phrases slowly. This is usually the hardest stage, and it deserves the most time.

**2. Someone easy to love.** Bring to mind someone whose presence reliably warms you: a friend, a child, a dog. Offer the same phrases to them. Notice how much easier it flows.

**3. Someone neutral.** Someone you neither love nor dislike: the person at the till, a neighbour you nod to. Offer them the phrases too.

**4. Someone difficult.** Someone with whom things are not easy. Be gentle with yourself here; choose someone mildly irritating rather than someone who has hurt you badly. This stage is practice at the edge, not a feat of strength. If it is too much, return to the earlier stages.

**5. All beings.** Widen the phrases outwards: everyone in the building, the town, further still. "May all beings be safe. May all beings be well."

## What to expect

The honest answer is: anything. Some sessions feel warm and open from the first phrase. More often there is resistance: boredom, embarrassment, a loud inner voice saying this is nonsense, a wave of sadness where you expected kindness. All of it is the practice. You are not trying to manufacture a feeling. You are repeating an intention and watching, with full awareness, what your heart does with it.

> A student once complained that after weeks of practice they felt no love at all. Their teacher asked: "And who is it that has been sitting there noticing the absence of love all this time?" Keep going. The noticing is the practice.
