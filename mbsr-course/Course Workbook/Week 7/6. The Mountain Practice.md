# The mountain practice

This is a sitting practice built around an image. You might use it as your main formal practice this week, or at any point in the course when life feels stormy and you need something steadier to rest on.

## Why an image?

The breath and the body are reliable anchors, but an image gives the mind a fuller shape to inhabit, and there is no steadier image than a mountain. It sits. Weather arrives, exhausts itself, and moves on. Seasons circle it. The mountain is unmoved by none of it, simply because it is what it is and stays where it is.

## The practice

Sit in your usual meditation posture, and let the posture itself begin the image: a stable base, a long upright spine, the head balanced and still. Then take yourself up the mountain in stages, with unhurried practice between each.

**Become the shape of it.** Picture a mountain: immense, its base rooted deep in the earth, its peak high above the tree line. Notice the mass of it. Then, breathing steadily, let the sitting body take on that shape. Your base is broad and grounded. Your upper body rises from it, settled and enormous.

**See the mountain's changes.** Day arrives on the mountain, and night. Clouds gather and burst; rain hammers the slopes and drains away. Snow falls and lies for months, then melts. Visitors climb up and come down again, and the mountain barely registers them. Notice that all of the movement belongs to the weather, and none of it to the mountain.

**Meet your own weather.** Now let the mountain stand for you and the weather stand for everything that moves through your life and your mind. Pressure at work, arguments, moods, plans, storms. Storms of thought, thunderstorms of feeling. Each one is real, and each one arrives, does its worst, and passes. And through all of it something in you, like the mountain, simply remains: aware, upright, unmoved at the base.

**Rest there.** Stay as the mountain for the rest of your sitting time. When your attention is blown away by a gust, come back, exactly as you always do in practice. Let the weather be exactly as bad as the weather is. There is no forecast you have to fix from the mountain. There is just sitting here, the weather passing through, and the mountain, as always, remaining.

> Mountains survive all weather by not arguing with any of it.
