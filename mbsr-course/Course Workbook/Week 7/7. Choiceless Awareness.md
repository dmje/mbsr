# Choiceless awareness

Over these weeks your sitting practice has had somewhere to land: the breath, the body, sounds, thoughts, feelings. This practice removes the landing zone. Attention rests in awareness itself, and lets whatever arises, arise.

## From choosing to allowing

Every practice so far has been a decision about where to place attention. That is exactly as it should be: you cannot practise open awareness until you have practised steadying attention somewhere. By now you have a genuinely trained attention, and this practice is what it was for.

In choiceless awareness there is no object you select and no object you reject. Sounds, body sensations, thoughts, moods, the feeling of the room: each is allowed its moment and allowed to pass, and awareness receives all of it without reaching for any of it.

## The practice

Sit, and begin by grounding yourself in the breath for a few minutes, so the practice starts from steadiness rather than vagueness.

Then let go of the focus on the breath and simply wait, in a friendly way, for anything that comes. A sound; let it come. A thought; let it come and let it go. An itch, a feeling, a memory of lunch; same thing. You are not looking for anything in particular, and you are not sweeping anything away.

Two gradual refinements:

- **If everything goes quiet**, do not go digging for experience. Quiet is also an object of awareness. Rest in it.
- **If everything gets crowded** and you feel yourself being dragged, there is no failure in taking back the breath as an anchor for a while, then opening again.

## Some honest warnings

This is the most rewarding of the practices and the easiest to fake. It is worth being clear about two things. First, choiceless awareness is not letting your mind wander wherever it likes while you daydream; in choiceless awareness there is vivid, continuous presence. Second, you cannot succeed at being aware by trying hard at it; effort itself is just more weather.

If you sit for twenty minutes and only realise at the end that you spent nineteen of them planning the weekend, you have still practised the most important part: the moment you noticed.

> The earlier practices are like learning to hold one note. This is letting the whole song play.
