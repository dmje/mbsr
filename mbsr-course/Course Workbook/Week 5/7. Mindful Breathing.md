# Mindful breathing

Breathing is a fundamental part of our being. If we’re alive, we’re breathing - it is always with us, always there to be noticed, always right here in this moment. Our breath is a part of our body, it's a powerful “hook” or "anchor" that reminds us to bring our attention back into our bodies, to notice what is happening for us right now. Our breath can give us a sense of how we are holding ourselves. If we are flustered or activated our breath is shorter and shallower, and if we are calm and more relaxed, our breath is smoother and deeper. 

Focused attention on the breath not only calms us down, but as the mind settles down it allows different thoughts, emotions, or sensations in the body to bubble up into awareness, to come out into the open; experiences that perhaps we hadn't seen before, or perhaps we hadn't wanted to see. And it gives us an opportunity to really see and understand them, without pushing them away or struggling against them.

For this practice then, we take the breath as our focus of attention, grounding awareness in the simple concrete sensations of breathing in the body. Without trying to do anything with the breath, but just letting the breath be natural, we rest the mind on the physical sensations of breathing, exploring it and becoming curious. We allow the breath to be our anchor bringing us back each time we are distracted. 

## Posture

[ seated position image]

Mindful breathing is best done in a sitting posture. Find a position that is comfortable to you. You don’t have to be cross-legged and you certainly don’t need to adopt a full or half Lotus! - it’s fine if you do, but your comfort is important, as you will be spending a decent period of time in this same position.

It’s ok to sit in a chair - if you do, put your feet flat on the floor; don’t cross your ankles, finding a sense of being grounded by the contact between your feet and the floor. 

Be upright. Imagine a thin thread pulling you up by the crown of your head, aligning your tailbone, your spine, and head. This imaginary thread would be taught but not tense; your back should be straight but not uncomfortably so. 

Your knees should be below your hips. Many meditators use a cushion or similar to achieve this position. Sometimes it takes several cushions or pillows - you can sit astride these or have them so they support the lower part of your spine, or however you like - just do what works for you and your body. 

Some other meditators use a stool - you sit astride it and tilt forwards, keeping your knees below your hips, extending your legs as needed. 

Your chin should be retracted but not down on your chest as this will make you drowsy. We suggest you focus on a spot on the floor a couple of feet in front of you before closing your eyes or you can keep them open.
