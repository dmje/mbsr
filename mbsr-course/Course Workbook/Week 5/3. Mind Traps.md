# Mind Traps
 
The quick and dirty nature of our reactions, as well as their independence from conscious reasoning, means that they are prone to all sorts of muddled thinking, biases of both emotional and cognitive sorts, and negative thought-patterns that can constantly alter our mood. These conditioned responses have slowly developed in us from as early as childhood, which we might more generally call 'mind traps'.


## Mind Traps in relation to self

| Mind Traps              | Example                                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Catastrophizing         | “I won't be able to get this done on time... then we’ll lose the house if I can’t keep this job.” |
| Perfectionism           | ‘I shouldn’t make mistakes.’ 'I can't fail.'                                                                         |
| Eternalising            | ‘I’ll always feel like this.' 'I'll never be like that.'                                                   |
| All or nothing thinking | ‘There's no way I can do this.' ‘If I don’t finish my to-do list today I’ve failed.’           |
| Negative self-talk      | ‘What kind of idiot says that!' 'How could I mess that up? I really am hopeless.’                                                          |
| Crystal ball gazing     | ‘I’m definitely not going to enjoy this.’                                                                       |


## Mind Traps in relation to others

| Mind Traps                   | Example                                                                                |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Comparing yourself to others | ‘She never seems stressed out like me.' 'I'm definitely doing better than they are.’ |
| Mind-reading                 | ‘They clearly don’t like me.' 'I can tell they are laughing at me.’                    |
| Being the external expert    | 'I already know that.' 'Well actually, it's more like this...'                                                                                   |
| Blaming                      | '*They* made me feel like this.'                                                                                   |
