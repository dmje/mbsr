# Awareness of thoughts and emotions

Your sitting practice so far has mostly used the breath and the body as its home base. Now the practice widens: the thoughts and feelings themselves become things you can be aware of, rather than things that claim you.

## Why this matters

Most of the time we do not experience our thoughts as events; we experience the world through them. An anxious thought about tomorrow arrives, and it is as if tomorrow has already gone wrong. A self-critical thought arrives, and it simply registers as fact.

Mindfulness of thoughts is the practice of watching thoughts arrive and pass, like weather. Not because they are unimportant, but because you see what they actually are: events in awareness, sometimes useful, frequently untrue, and none of them permanent.

## The practice, in stages

Sit as you usually do, and begin with the breath. Then widen the field of attention.

**Stage one: notice the pull.** At some point you will realise you have been swept away by a train of thought. That moment of realisation is the whole practice in miniature. Instead of treating it as failure, note it: "thinking." Then return to the breath.

**Stage two: watch thoughts arrive and pass.** Instead of returning to the breath straight away, let some of your attention rest on the process of thinking itself. Watch as a thought surfaces, forms, is recognised, and dissolves. Try labelling a few: "planning," "remembering," "worrying," "rehearsing." Labelling is not judging; it is naming the weather.

**Stage three: add the feelings.** When a thought carries an emotional charge, sadness or excitement or dread, see if you can find the feeling in the body as well as the story in the mind. Where is it? How does it feel? Here you are practising the same turning towards you learned this week.

**Stage four: rest as the observer.** Some sits you will glimpse the difference between the weather and the sky. Thoughts and emotions move across awareness; awareness itself does not move. Do not strive for this. If it happens, notice it. If it does not, the noticing of thought after thought after thought is a complete practice.

## Two things to expect

First, it feels strange at first to watch your own thinking, a little like standing behind yourself in a queue. It settles with practice. Second, you will be told by your own mind, in your own voice, that you are doing it wrong. That is a thought too. You can watch that one as well.
