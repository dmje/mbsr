# What is a ‘Reaction’?
 
We have already, and will continue to speak a lot about ‘reactions’, both in the mind and the body, so it is worth spending a little time clarifying what exactly we mean by this general term.
 
Roughly, we can think of a reaction as an automatic and immediate response to an event, absent any voluntary control. 

### Key Points

- We do not choose our reactions, they are automatic. Reactions do not follow from conscious deliberation or rationality, they are quick and dirty, providing the most immediate and cheapest way for the brain to respond to an event. They are incredibly useful in helping us to avoid perilous danger with only a split-second to respond, but not so useful in the modern world where navigating complex relationships demand a more considered and intentional attitude. 
- When we react to events the brain draws on information from the amygdala. This information contains unconscious, deeply-habituated, irrational patterns of thought as well as highly emotional content that evoke implicit memories from as early as childhood (Hanson, 2009).
- Reactions are not single, they often occur in chains tumbling us forward into the future. As they are based on deeply ingrained psychological habits and seductively emotional content, this poses a special challenge in finding the space to unhook and calm the mind.
- We are reacting a lot of the time. Reactions thrive off unawareness and they can be quite subtle, particularly in the body, they need not be fight or flight. For example, when we are on autopilot even something as simple as bumping into a neighbour that we don’t particularly like on our way out of the house can trigger a chain of aversive thoughts and emotions. Unless we bring these reactions into awareness then they can snowball into even more emotional states of mind.
 
Imagine… You’ve just come home from a long and tiring day at work to find the house in a state of complete disarray. You remember that your partner promised you yesterday that they would clean the place up... but wait… yep, you find them on the sofa watching TV. 

Perhaps your experience unfolds something like this:

- Your shoulders immediately tighten up.
- “I can’t believe it”
- “There’s no way I’m spending another evening cleaning.”
- You feel a sinking feeling in your stomach.
- “This always happens, why am I always expected to do everything around here.”
- Your face flushes and you start to feel hot.
- “Why doesn’t anybody respect me or consider my feelings?”
- Your heart rate quickens and you feel a tightness and pressure in your chest.
- “They clearly don’t care about me at all!”
