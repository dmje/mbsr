2.  Practice 'Taking In The Good' this week.

Indulge yourself in the simple joys of life. The sound of birds, a smile on the face of a loved one, the colour of the sky at dusk, the smell of a flower, a simple moment of connection with a stranger, the smell of fresh coffee, the taste of a meal you enjoy, the feeling when you put on an episode of your favourite TV show.

Whatever it is, we are giving you permission to really enjoy it! 

Just like meditation the more you practice, the more the brain rewires itself around these more positive, nourishing mental states. Although at first it takes deliberate cultivation, over time, mind states like gratitude and joy for the little things in life will become more natural spaces the mind naturally wanders in and out of. So just keep at it!

You could try writing a gratitude journal if you find that sort of thing helpful. Note down a few things that you are grateful for each day. Just remember to really feel that gratitude, give yourself space and time to let the experience surface and soak in it.

## Meditation is the training ground

Meditation, as well as being a space for bringing stillness and self-care into our lives, is also a rigorous training ground. It provides us with a safe space to observe our minds; its habitual ways of reacting to pleasant and unpleasant experiences, the kinds of stories we repeatedly get lost in, as well as revealing to us over and over again the changing, impermanent nature of all experience. The mind that shows up on the cushion is the very same mind that we bring with us out into the world. The practice therefore, is like a training ground for the rest of our lives. In it we practice openness and non-reactivity, we break old habits, build new habits, we develop wisdom, compassion and insight, we cultivate new attitudes of mindfulness.  

Our time on the cushion paves the way for nothing short of a revolution in our way of living, one with greater presence of mind, a deeper sense of ease and understanding and a hell of a lot less stress. So practice! With gentleness, but also with great diligence and determination.
