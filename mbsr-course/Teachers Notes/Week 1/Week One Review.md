# Week One Review

Let’s start Week Two with a review of the work we’ve done together already.

Firstly, think about how the body scan went for you over the last 7 days. It’s useful to review your daily notes and also to write down any impressions you have overall. Use some of these questions as prompts:  

- What did you notice during the body scan?
- Did any particular impressions stay with you?
- What happened when your mind wandered – how did you react?

Once you’ve made your notes, reflect on them for a moment. Notice where you have been over-critical or harsh with yourself.

It is extremely common at this stage for the overwhelming feeling to be something along the lines of: “my god, my mind is so incredibly scattered and busy – I found it nearly impossible to concentrate!“. In fact, I’d be extremely surprised if this wasn’t your experience too. Hugely experienced meditators with thousands of hours of practice under their belts still find the same – sometimes, the monkey mind just…. monkeys about.

*The mind is like a time machine: always in the past or future, and rarely in the present*

The single most important thing to take on board about this is exactly this point: this is what everyone finds – you are normal!. You aren’t a terrible meditator, you aren’t a lesser human, you’re not “doing it wrong” if your mind wanders all the time. You’re just a busy, normal, thinking person!

We’re learning to concentrate here – and believe me, it does get better – but we’re also learning compassion, for others, but importantly – for ourselves. Remember Sharon Salzburg’s excellent advice – the moment when you notice that your mind has wandered is the moment of the practice. Use your “wander noticing” as a positive thing to boost your practice, not as a stick to beat yourself up!

When you’re early on with your meditation practice, you’ll find that the prompt to return back to the practice often comes from your teacher or the recording you’re listening to. As you spend more time doing this and become more adept, this prompt will start to come from within yourself as well – you’ll find yourself returning much more easily to the practice and the time you spend focused within the practice will grow.

Let’s now reflect on the informal practice during the week. Again, make some notes or refer to your journal and ask similar questions of yourself:  
  
-   How did my informal practice go?
-   What did I notice?
-   What prompts helped me during the week?

You’ll find that the formal practice will not only improve your abilities and concentration with the formal side of things, but will also increase your awareness when you aren’t formally sitting. As we said before – the formal practice is a tool to help us get better at life, not an end in itself.
